Origin Story

Iraq, 2006

I was deployed in Iraq, running convoy security missions and trying to figure out what came next. I knew I wanted to go to business school, but I had no money, no network in finance, and no clear path forward.

So I did something uncomfortable. I started cold-calling West Point graduates I'd never met. I pulled names from the alumni directory, found their contact information, and reached out. Most didn't respond. Some were polite but couldn't help. But a few—just a few—took the time to talk.

"One of those conversations led to an introduction. That introduction led to Columbia Business School on a full scholarship. That degree changed my entire trajectory."

I didn't realize it at the time, but I had stumbled onto something powerful: a shared institutional bond plus a genuine reason to connect is not a favor—it's a repeatable system. The problem is, most of us never systematically work that system. We let relationships go dormant. We don't know who to call, what to say, or how to follow up. We leave enormous value on the table.

I built Victory Vision because I needed it. And if you're reading this, you probably do too.
