The Origination-to-Execution Gap

Why Being First to See a Deal Means Nothing If You're Third to Sign

You had the seller's attention before anyone else. You asked the right questions. You understood the business. But by the time you circulated the investment memo internally, two other funds had already submitted LOIs.

The origination-to-execution gap is the silent killer most funds ignore. Sourcing advantage evaporates when your internal process takes 45 days and your competitor moves in 14. In a constrained origination environment, the cost of losing velocity is not just one deal — it's the compounding effect of watching your pipeline convert at half the rate of faster competitors.

The three-phase pre-qualification protocol addresses the exact bottleneck: how to compress diligence without increasing risk, using the same pattern-recognition discipline you learned at the academy.

Pattern Recognition Under Pressure

How Academy Training in Pattern Recognition Under Time Pressure Applies Directly to Seller Pre-Qualification

At West Point, you were trained to make sound decisions with incomplete data under time constraints. You learned to identify the critical variables, assess risk rapidly, and commit to a course of action while others were still gathering information.

That same discipline is the commercial analog to seller pre-qualification. The framework is not about cutting corners — it's about knowing which questions eliminate 80% of risk in the first conversation, which data points predict post-close performance, and how to structure your internal workflow so that speed becomes a competitive weapon rather than a liability.

Most funds treat every opportunity the same way: full diligence, full committee process, full timeline. The protocol teaches you to tier opportunities in the first 72 hours and allocate resources accordingly, so that when you identify a high-conviction target, you can move with the speed that wins mandates.

Case Study: 24-Day Close

What Jason Gonzalez Did Differently to Close in 24 Days This Year When Competitors Took 90

Jason Gonzalez, Class of 2012, closed a $12M platform acquisition in 24 days in Q1 2026. His competitors — three other sub-$500M funds — were still in diligence when he wired funds. The difference was not capital availability, sector expertise, or seller relationship. It was process.

Jason used the three-phase pre-qualification protocol to compress his timeline without increasing risk. He identified the three critical diligence questions in the first call, structured his LOI to de-risk the seller's concerns upfront, and ran parallel workstreams instead of sequential gates. By the time his competitors scheduled their second management meeting, Jason had already closed.

The protocol does not require a larger team, more capital, or proprietary deal flow. It requires a different operating system — one that treats speed as a strategic advantage and builds the infrastructure to execute on it consistently.
