The Problem

The Origination-to-Execution Gap

You have deal flow. You have relationships. You see opportunities before most of the market does. But when the seller signs, it's not your name on the LOI.

The problem isn't sourcing. It's not capital. It's not sector expertise. The problem is that you're treating pre-qualification as a diligence phase instead of a velocity advantage.

Being first to see a deal means nothing if you're third to sign. In a constrained origination environment, the firms that win are the ones who can move from initial conversation to executable term sheet while competitors are still scheduling their second meeting.

Three Critical Insights

Why Speed Beats Access

1. Why Being First to See a Deal Means Nothing If You're Third to Sign

Most independent sponsors and small fund managers believe their competitive advantage is access. They cultivate relationships with intermediaries, industry operators, and business owners. They get the call before the deal goes to market.

Then they lose the transaction to a competitor who showed up three weeks later but moved faster.

The origination-to-execution gap is the silent killer of deal flow. You spend months building the relationship that gets you the first conversation. Then you spend weeks "getting comfortable" with the opportunity while a competitor runs a 10-day pre-qualification sprint and submits an LOI before you've finished your sector analysis.

In 2026, with fewer quality transactions available, the firms that win are the ones who treat pre-qualification as a speed discipline, not a diligence phase. The three-phase protocol gives you a systematic method to move from initial conversation to executable term sheet in days, not weeks.

2. How Academy Training Applies to Seller Pre-Qualification

West Point teaches you to make sound decisions under time pressure with incomplete information. You learn to assess a situation, identify the critical variables, and act while others are still gathering data.

That's exactly the skill required for pre-qualification in a competitive deal environment.

The commercial analog isn't running a full diligence process before you submit an LOI. It's identifying the three to five variables that determine whether a transaction is executable, validating those variables in the first 72 hours, and moving to term sheet while competitors are still building their financial model.

The three-phase protocol maps directly to the pattern-recognition framework you already know: Assess (identify the critical variables), Validate (confirm or disqualify in 72 hours), Execute (move to term sheet before the window closes).

Your academy training is a competitive advantage. You just need a commercial framework that lets you apply it.

3. What Jason Gonzalez Did Differently to Close in 24 Days

Jason Gonzalez, USMA '09, is an independent sponsor who saw three deals in 2025 and closed zero. In early 2026, he implemented the three-phase pre-qualification protocol.

In March 2026, he received an introduction to a lower-middle-market services business. First conversation on a Tuesday. Pre-qualification complete by Friday. LOI submitted the following Monday. Transaction closed 24 days later.

His competitors—two regional funds and another independent sponsor—were still in the "getting to know you" phase when the seller accepted Jason's term sheet.

What Jason did differently: He treated pre-qualification as a velocity discipline. He identified the three critical variables (owner motivation, financial sustainability, and management transition risk) in the first call. He validated all three in 72 hours. He moved to LOI while competitors were still scheduling their second meeting.

The three-phase protocol doesn't replace diligence. It replaces the weeks of unstructured "exploration" that most sponsors mistake for relationship-building. Jason built the relationship by moving fast and demonstrating competence, not by dragging out the courtship.
